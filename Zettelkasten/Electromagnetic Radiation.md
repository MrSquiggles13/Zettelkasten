# Electromagnetic Radiation

## Notes

Electromagnetic radiation is the energy propagated by perpendicular electric and magnetic fields that increase and decrease in intensity as they move through space in waves. Electromagnetic radiation is also known as light and is classified by the measure of its wavelength or frequency and is further classified in groups of similar light properties.

Wavelength is the measure of the distance between crests of an oscillation and is denoted by the symbol $\lambda$. Frequency is the measure of cycles of oscillations per second a wave moves through a point in space and is inversely related to wavelength and denoted by $v$. Since light always moves at a constant speed c = 299,792,458 m/s $\lambda + v = c$. This formula can be used to find wavelength with a known frequency and vice versa. Amplitude (A) is the intensity or for visible light the brightness of the electromagnetic radiation.

Light of a single wavelength is called monochromatic while light which consists of multiple wavelength is called polychromatic.

A photon is the smallest packet of energy for a particular wavelength. The energy of a photon is $E_{photon} = \frac{hc}{\lambda} = hv$ and also the change of energy when an electron excites to another shell $E_{photon} = |\Delta E_{electron}|$.

When elements are heated or electrically charged they emit specific wavelengths of electromagnetic radiation. This can be measured and observed through atomic spectra where the light emitted is focused through a prism and will split it into its specific wavelengths. Hydrogen line spectrum is the energy levels hydrogen gives off and have four lines of visible light wavelengths (410nm, 434nm, 486nm, 656nm).

When light or any wave-like movement strikes an edge it bends around that edge which is called diffraction. When waves of light pass through two adjacent slits the emerging circular waves interact through a process called interference and form a diffraction pattern. Waves that coincide or are in phase interfere constructively and summate each other's amplitudes while waves whose troughs and crests coincide or out of phase interfere destructively and the amplitudes cancel each other out. This forms a diffraction pattern where the brighter areas are the more constructive interference and the darker areas are the more destructive interference.

When electrons transfer to lower orbitals they emit certain types of electromagnetic radiation. When an electron drops down to n = 1 energy level they emit UV radiation, visible radiation when dropped to n = 2 and infrared radiation when dropped down to n = 3.

Spectroscopy and spectrometry are techniques that obtain light spectra in order to gather data on a substance's atomic and molecular energy levels. Elements and compounds have unique line spectra which act as a fingerprint for identifying them. 

Photons sometime act as particles like when they transfer momentum when striking carbon's electrons in graphite form. Shorter wavelengths of light have greater momentum shown by the equation $p = \frac{h}{\lambda}$. On a macroscopic level it makes sense that energy acts as waves while matter acts as particles but on the microscopic/atomic/quantum level this no longer holds true. Energy and matter both act like waves and particles depending on the situation and this observation is called the wave-particle duality.
