# Electricity

## Notes

Electricity is the process of electrons freely being exchanged in a polar direction through a material. Electricity's properties can be measured with certain values:
	1. Coulomb - The measure of charge; one Coulomb approximately equals ![](data:image/gif;base64,R0lGODlhAQABAIAAAP///wAAACH5BAEAAAAALAAAAAABAAEAAAICRAEAOw==)6.24 ×10 ^ 18 electrons
	2. Voltage - The potential difference of a power source which is measured in volts which equals 1 joule per Coulomb
	3. Current - How many electrons are passing through a defined area per unit time. Measured in Amperes or Amps (A) which 1A = 1 Coulomb per second
	4. Resistance - The slowing or lessening of current is measure by Ohms
Resistors can come in many forms like LEDs or small components with a semi inductive material. Components are conductors if they allow the flow of electrons through the material and inductors if they resist that flow.

Circuits are a collection of wires and components that can hold electrical current. Circuits are closed if there is no break in the current flow and open if there is a break.

Components in a system can be wired either in parallel where a split in the wire holds different components then meets again or in series where the components are all on the same wire. When measuring voltage the connectors must be in parallel but when measuring current they have to be in series with the circuit.

When measuring resistance if the resistors are in series the resistance can be summated while if the resistors are in parallel they are calculated by the formula $\frac{1}{R_{\text{total}}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3} + \dots + \frac{1}{R_n}$.

![[Pasted image 20260406030743.png|270]]
Ohm's Law states that the current through a resistor when measured between two points is proportional to the potential difference (voltage) and inversely proportional to the resistance between the points. 

Kirchhoff's Voltage Law states that the net charge in voltage around a closed loop is zero as a consequence of the conservation of energy. This can be used to find the voltage across a resistor.

Kirchhoff's Current Law states that the sum of current that enters a node will equal the sum of current leaving a node. Current can be calculated for each path of a circuit as they will add towards the total initial current.
