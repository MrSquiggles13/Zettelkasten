# Decision Matrix

## Notes

Utilized for comparing different design ideas by their weighted criteria. Criteria should be comparable between designs and quantified even if not traditionally measurable. Choosing criteria should be based on the data that has been collected modelled and analyzed. To calculate weights for the decision matrix an Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) should be utilized. In an AHP each criterion is compared to one other criterion and given a score of 1-10 where 1 is equally desirable and 10 is definitively favorable to the former criterion. In the corresponding square where the individual criterion are compared but in the reverse the reciprocal of the store is stored. After all the scores are factored they are added up in each row and the subsequent row total is notated. A percentage is calculated using those totals and that percentage is the weight of each criterion.

Here we can see that Cost, Weight, Looks, Repair are the criteria and Weight ironically has the most weight compared to other criteria. It can also be seen that the weight for each criterion is very close to the others which suggests that they all have somewhat equal magnitude to deciding on a design.

After an AHP is made to calculate the weights of the criteria those weights can be used in the Decision Matrix.

Decision Matrix Example:
![[Pasted image 20260320030829.png]]

The designs are given their own rating (typically 1-5) for each criterion and that rating is given a score which is rating X weight. The scores are totaled for each design and those total scores are compared. The highest score shows the design that best accommodates the weighted criteria.

Though the Decision Matrix is a good tool to use it should not be the explanation of why the design was chosen. The decision matrix is only to organize the data it is up to the engineer to explain how the ratings weights and decisions were actually made. This also goes to show that the design chosen does not have to be the final solution as better solutions could arise or criteria revaluated and weights recalculated.
