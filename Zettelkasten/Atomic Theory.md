# Atomic Theory

## Notes

Dalton originally proposed that
1. All matter is composed of small indivisible particles called atoms
2. All atoms of a given element are identical in mass and properties
3. Atoms cannot be created nor destroyed
4. Atoms combine in fixed whole number ratios to form compounds

Dalton had the first 2 ideas wrong and were corrected to
1. All matter is composed of atoms which consist of protons electrons and neutrons
2. All atoms of a given element have the same number of protons and most properties are identical

Atoms are made up of subatomic particles called
- Electrons - negatively charged and are the smallest
- Protons - Positively charged and are similar in size to neutrons
- Neutrons - Have no charge and are similar in size to protons

The discovery of electrons involved an experiment conducted by J.J. Thomson where a glass tube evacuated of almost all gas had metal electrodes fixed on both ends. One end was coated in phosphor which emits light when exposed to electrical current. When the electrodes were powered rays could be seen striking the phosphor coating. These rays were called cathode rays since the emitted from the cathode (negative) side to the anode (positive) side and were confirmed to be electrons.

The cathode ray tube experiment also helped calculate the mass of electrons which proved not only how small they were but that atoms were made up of even smaller parts.

Another experiment was conducted using fine oil droplets x-rays and metal plates to calculate the charge of an electron which was derived from the previous mass experiment.

Rutherford proved through shooting alpha particles at a substance that a central nucleus must be present in atoms and that nucleus is made up of protons (also neutrons but proven later). The atomic nucleus makes up only 0.0001 of the volume of an atom yet makes up 99.9% of the mass

The amount of protons are what dictate the type of element of an atom and is also the atomic number. A neutral atom will have the same amount of electrons as protons.

The Bohr model is based on the hydrogen line spectrum and describes an atom in a planetary nature with the nucleus at the center and concentric rings or orbits that are at certain radial intervals away from the nucleus. Bohr was also able to discover that electrons emitted a specific energy quantity when shifting orbits through this model. The lines or wavelengths of light that the H line spectrum was showing were the energy level thresholds that hydrogen emits when electrons move down in orbit but these were the only ones in the visible light spectrum as other orbit shifts showed light outside the visible light spectrum. Bohr's model lacked a configuration for multi-electron atoms and their spectra while also not accounting for the wave-particle behavior of electrons. Planck was later able to discover these quantized energy levels in multi electron atoms and called them quantum. Bohr's model also led to the energy of an atom equation when $E = -2.18 \cdot 10^{-18}J(\frac{z^2}{n^2})$ where Z is the charge and n is the energy state.

Atomic theory evolved over time as it was debated over whether light was a wave or a particle. An experiment involving heating objects with electromagnetic radiation showed that the classical wave model could not find a relationship between hot blackbody's energy released and the wavelength of the radiation. Th photoelectric effect where monochromatic light of sufficient frequency is shone on a metal plate which make a current flow, was originally hypothesized that the free electrons on the metal's surface was receiving energy from the light but it was realized that the frequency not the amplitude of the light was the contributing factor to the current flow and that the frequency had to be at certain discrete limits called frequency thresholds. Also the lack of a time lag between the light shone on the metal and the current flowing showed that the electrons were not building up energy to an excited state but were excited immediately by the light. This led to the discovery of photons or quantized small groups of light energy.

The modern atomic model is based on Bohr's findings but show electrons travelling in probability fields called orbitals instead of fixed planetary like orbits. Orbitals are three dimensional regions in space where an electron has a high probability to be found using Schrodinger's wave function which when squared is the probability density.

It was later discovered that mass also moves like waves which was proven by observing the frequencies of fixed oscillations of objects (like a guitar string) and their whole value metric of the division of the object's length with the oscillation. An equation was later derived to show this relationship of mass and wavelength from Einstein's $E=mc^2$ and Planck's $E=\frac{hc}{\lambda}$ equating each other and simplifying to $\lambda = \frac{h}{mc}$ where c can be replaced by $u$ which relates to any velocity not just the speed of light's. This shows an inverse relationship of wavelength to mass and why macroscopic objects like a baseball being thrown will have unobservable wavelengths since they will be extremely small.
