# Electrochemistry

## Notes

Electrochemistry is the study of the relationship between chemical change and electrical work.

Batteries are electrochemical cells or systems that incorporate an oxidation-reduction reaction to produce or utilize electrical energy.

Half reaction method is used for oxidation-reduction reactions and involves dividing the overall reaction into oxidation and reduction half reactions. This is more equivalent to how a battery functions and helps determine the oxidation and reduction agents. The half reaction method starts with a skeleton ionic reaction equation consisting only of the components that are oxidized and reduced. These are divided into separate reactions, half-reactions, that are each paired with their oxidized and reduced component over the reaction i.e. $2Br^- \rightarrow Br_2$. Then the reaction is balanced in this order: All atoms are balanced except for O and H by adding coefficients where needed, balancing O by adding H<sub>2</sub>O molecules, balancing H with H<sup>+</sup> ions, balance charge by adding electrons, then if necessary multiplying one or both half-reactions by an integer so the electrons gained in the reduction equals the electrons lost in the oxidation. Then the half-reactions are put together as one reaction keeping their respective sides and cancelling out any components that are on both sides of the equation. For basic solutions OH<sup>-</sup> is added to bother sides of the chemical equation for every H<sup>+</sup> and the side with both combine into H<sub>2</sub>O.

Voltaic or galvanized cells use spontaneous oxidation-reduction reactions to generate electrical energy. In the cell's reaction some of the difference in Gibbs energy between the higher energy reactants and the lower energy products is converted to electrical energy. While a displacement oxidation-reduction reaction can happen in the same container a voltaic cell requires that each substance is separated into different beakers and connected by an external circuit so electron transfer can happen generating current. These separated half-reactions are called half-cells and contain an electrolyte that can carry charge and an electrode that is a solid which can conduct electrons into or out of the cell. 

The oxidation half-cell contains the anode electrode where electrons that are lost leave through and the reduction half-cell contains the cathode where the electrons that are gained enter through. The external circuit that joins the two half-cells is where voltage can be measured. In any voltaic cell the anode is negative relative to the cathode and the electrons transfer from anode to cathode. 

A salt bridge helps complete the circuit by providing the anions and cations to neutralize each half-cell otherwise no current can flow since the build up of either ions will either repel or attract the electrons just gained or lost and will not allow a flow. Anions move into the anode half-cell from the salt bridge to neutralize the excess oxidized cations produced and cations move into the cathode half-cell from the salt bridge to neutralize the left over anion electrolytes that the reduced cations left behind.

Active electrodes are made of components that are in the half-reaction and will lose (anode) or gain (cathode) mass as it participates in the reaction. Inactive electrodes do not participate in the reaction but still transfer electrons from the oxidation half-cell to the reduction half-cell to complete the circuit and are used when an active electrode is not physically possible or practical to use and most are either platinum or graphite.

Voltaic cell notation helps show the reactions with all its components - 
anode | phase || <-(salt bridge) phase, same phase | different phase | cathode.

The flow of electrons is determined by which electrolyte is a stronger reducing agent because it is more readily able to lose electrons to displace a less active substance. The difference in potential energy of both substances to oxidize is the potential difference or electric potential and those differences equalizing is what generates current or electron flow.

Cell potential (E<sub>cell</sub>), Voltage (V), or electromotive force (emf) is the difference in electric potential between two electrodes and is proportional to the kinetic energy of electrons flowing through a circuit also called electrical energy. Electron flow is spontaneous from the negative to the positive electrode or the more positive cell potential. When E<sub>cell</sub> > 0 then the reaction is spontaneous and the more positive the cell potential the more work that cell can do and the reaction proceeds farther to the right. When E<sub>cell</sub> < 0 then the reaction is nonspontaneous and when E<sub>cell</sub> = 0 then the reaction is at equilibrium and the cell can no longer do work. The magnitude of E<sub>cell</sub> id dependent on the concentration of the electrolytes and the temperature.

Electrical potential has the SI unit of volt (V) and charge has the SI unit of coulomb (C). If two electrodes differ by one volt of electrical potential then one joule of energy is released (on joule of work can be done) for each coulomb of charge that moves between the electrodes $1V = 1 \frac{J}{C}$.

Standard cell potential E°<sub>cell</sub> is the cell potential at a specific temperature and under standard conditions (1 M, 1 atm, etc.). Standard electrode (or half-cell) potential E°<sub>half-cell</sub> is the electrical potential of a given half-reaction with its components in standard state. Standard half-cell potentials refer to half-reactions written as reductions. These values can be compared between half-cells to determine which is the cathode (more negative E°<sub>half-cell</sub>) and the anode (more positive E°<sub>half-cell</sub>). To find the total $E°_{cell} = E°_{cathode} (reduction) - E°_{anode} (oxidation)$ and if E°<sub>cell</sub> > 0 then the reaction is spontaneous at standard conditions.

An unknown E°<sub>half-cell</sub> can be compared to the reference half-cell E°<sub>reference</sub>. The E°<sub>reference</sub> is called the standard hydrogen electrode (SHE) and all half-cell potentials are relative to it. It consists of a platinum electrode that has H<sub>2</sub> gas at 1 atm bubbling through it while immersed in 1 M of strong acid (H<sup>+</sup>) $2H^+ (aq, 1M) + 2e^- \leftrightarrow H_2 (g, 1atm)$ and E°<sub>reference</sub> = 0.000 V. SHE can be either an anode (H<sub>2</sub> oxidized) or a cathode (H<sup>+</sup> reduced) depending on the other half-cell. When cathode and anode are identified can use the standard cell potential equation to determine the value of the unknown half-cell.

Cell potentials can be used to compare the relative strengths of oxidation and reduction agents. When cell potential is more positive it is a better oxidation agent or more easily reduced while more negative mean it is a better reduction agent or more easily oxidized. Since oxidation-reduction reactions are the sum of two half reactions there is an oxidation and reduction agent on the product and reactant sides. The strong oxidation and reduction agents spontaneously react to form weaker oxidation and reduction agents. For spontaneous oxidation-reduction reactions the half reaction with a higher E°<sub>half-cell</sub> proceeds at the cathode as a reduction reactant while the half reactions with a lower E°<sub>half-cell</sub> proceeds at the anode in reverse as an oxidation product.

A metal's activity strength can be measured by taking its half reaction and pairing it with the SHE and measuring its E°<sub>cell</sub> where if it is positive the metal can displace H<sub>2</sub> and the more positive the more active the metal is in comparison and a more negative E°<sub>half-cell</sub>. If E°<sub>cell</sub> is negative then the metal is less active and has a more positive E°<sub>half-cell</sub>. Some metals can displace H<sub>2</sub> from water and have a negative E°<sub>half-cell</sub> lower than that half reaction $2H_2O (l) + 2e^- \rightarrow H_2 (g) + 2OH^- (aq)$ where E° = - 0.828 V and have a positive E°<sub>cell</sub> when paired and a more negative E°<sub>half-cell</sub>.

Since Gibbs energy and electrical energy of a cell can both determine spontaneity and $\Delta G = w_{max} = -E_{cell} \cdot charge$ then we can substitute charge $charge = mol \ e^- \cdot \frac{charge}{mole \ e^-} = nF$ where $n = moles$ and $F = Faraday's \ constant = 96.485 \frac{coulombs}{mol \ e^-} = 96,485 \frac{J}{V \cdot mol \ e^-}$ so equating Gibbs energy $\Delta G = -nFE_{cell}$ also $\Delta G° = -nFE°_{cell}$ and since $\Delta G° = -RTln(K) = -nFE°_{cell}$ we can solve for $E°_{cell} = \frac{RT}{nF}ln(K)$ or combining the constants and converting to standard log $E°_{cell} = \frac{0.0592 V}{n}log(K)$ or $log(K) = \frac{nE°_{cell}}{0.0592 V}$ when @ 298 K.

For E<sub>cell</sub> in nonstandard conditions we can use $\Delta G = \Delta G° + RTln(Q)$ and subbing for delta G $-nFE_{cell} = -nFE°_{cell} + RTln(Q)$ simplified $E_{cell} = E°_{cell} - \frac{RT}{nF}ln(Q)$ combining constants and converting to standard log $E_{cell} = E°_{cell} - \frac{0.0592 V}{n}log(Q)$ @ 298 K. When Q < 1 E<sub>cell</sub> > E°<sub>cell</sub> and Q > 1 E<sub>cell</sub> < E°<sub>cell</sub> and Q = 1 E<sub>cell</sub> = E°<sub>cell</sub>.

As cells operate their potential decreases since the products grow and the reactants shrink causing Q to get larger which cause E<sub>cell</sub> to decrease until it reaches equilibrium or E<sub>cell</sub> = 0  and Q = K.

A concentration cell is two half-reactions of the same species but at different concentrations. The drive towards equilibrium or both concentrations equaling each other causes the diluted half-cell to be the anode since it wants to give up electrons to raise the concentration of the electrolyte solution with positive ions and the concentrated half-cell to be the cathode since it wants to gain electrons to become more diluted and convert the positive ions in the solution to solid plating.

Electrolysis is a process of splitting of a substance or causing a nonspontaneous reaction by input of electrical energy. Electrolysis of pure molten salts will reduce the cation and oxidize the anion. When there is a mixture of molten salts the metal cation with higher ionization energy will be reduced and the nonmetal anion with lower electronegativity is oxidized. The electrolysis of water into H<sub>2</sub> + O<sub>2</sub> where O oxidizes and H reduces. Aqueous salt solutions reductions happens to the less negative electrical potential electrode and oxidations happens to the less positive electrical potential electrode. 

An electrolytic cell uses electrical energy to drive a nonspontaneous reaction. When the supplied voltage is greater than the electric potential of the cell (E<sub>cell</sub>) the voltaic cells will reverse direction of the flow of electrons which makes the cathode the anode and the anode the cathode but their signs remain the same since electrons are supplied to the cathode and removed from the anode.

Gases have a high activation energy and need more than the expected voltage applied to form at the electrode, this extra voltage is called overvoltage. This can affect a mixed salt oxidation-reduction components at the electrode.

Corrosion is a natural oxidation-reduction process that causes the unwanted oxidation of a metal and has similar properties to a voltaic cell. Rust typically is caused by a defect in iron which comes in contact with water and is called the anodic region. This causes the iron to oxidize and the loose electrons to travel through the other regions of the iron where there is a high concentration of O<sub>2</sub>. This is called a cathodic region and will reduce the O<sub>2</sub> to water which creates a partial reaction that only forms water and Fe<sup>2+</sup> ions. These ions then react with the O<sub>2</sub> and form Fe<sup>3+</sup> ions that make rust or iron(III) oxide Fe<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>.

Since a balanced half reaction shows the stoichiometric relationship between the reactants, products and electrons. The amount of substances at each electrode is directly proportional to the amount of electrons and so is the charge flowing through the cell. Charge cannot be measured directly but current can which is charge per unit of time. The SI unit for current is the Ampere (A) or 1 A = 1 C/s where C = coulombs and s is seconds. This can be rearranged by multiplying current by seconds to give charge which can then be related to the amount of products.
