# Chemical Formulas and Equations

## Notes

A chemical equation is a statement that uses uses formulas to express the identities and quantities of substances involved in a chemical or physical change. A chemical formula is a representation of the types and amounts of elements in a chemical. 

A chemical equation consists of the reactants on the left side an arrow expressing the reaction and the products on the right side. Reactants are the substances before a change, products are the substances produced during the change.

In a chemical equation the chemical formulas also have a state of matter subscript representing each chemicals state of matter. These are represented in parenthesis after the chemical formula and are (g)as (l)iquid (s)olid and (aq)ueous.

A chemical equation must be balanced by having the same quantity of elements on the reactant and product side of the equation represented using the lowest whole numbers that satisfy the ratio. These coefficients are uses as a mole ratio or the amount of moles of each chemical in the equation and the ratio of moles of each reactant and product. The stoichiometric equivalent where the specific amount of one substance is formed from, produces or reacts with a specific amount of another.

Mass to mass conversion involves using the mass of one compound to calculate the mass of another compound in a chemical reaction by using the mole ratio and converting from gram to mole to gram.

The limiting reactant is a reactant that reacts completely and is not present when the chemical reaction completes and is the determining factor of how much product can form.

Percent yield is a percentage value of the actual yield over the theoretical yield. The actual yield is the measured amount produced from and experiment or process and the theoretical yield is the amount of product calculated assuming all the limited reactant reacts.

Example:
2Al<sub>(s)</sub> + Fe<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub><sub>(s)</sub> --> Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub><sub>(s)</sub> + 2Fe<sub>(l)</sub> : Al and Fe have coefficient of 2 to balance the equation and shows that for each two aluminum and one rust will produce one aluminum oxide and two iron.

### Empirical Formulas

An empirical formula represents the ratio of a molecular compound. Ionic compounds do not have an empirical formula.

Molecular formulas can be derived from the empirical formula by finding the mass of the compound and then dividing by the mass of the combined empirical formula elements and then multiplying the empirical formulas ratio by the dividend.
