# Scientific Method

## Notes

The scientific method is a process involving creative proposals and tests aimed at objective verifiable discoveries.

There are four main stages of the scientific method
1. Observation - facts that help explain the ideas that are contemplated. Quantitative observations or data is the most useful as it can show trends when analyzed. When an observation becomes universal with no clear exceptions that observation becomes a natural law.
2. Hypothesis - A proposal made to explain an observation. Hypotheses must be testable by an experiment and is often the reason for experiments. If not supported by the results of an experiment a hypothesis must be revised or discarded. Hypotheses can be altered but results cannot.
3. Experiment - A set of procedural steps to test a hypothesis which will normally lead to revision of the hypothesis and a new experiment for testing. Should contain at least 2 variable and only have one variable altered to measure the effect on another variable. All other variables should be constant and the experiment done in a controlled environment. Experiments must be reproducible and often require a combination of skill and creativity.
4. Model - Theories based on experiments that test hypotheses about observations. Models explain how a phenomenon occurs and is a simplified representation of some aspect of nature that is then used to predict other related phenomenon. Models can be refined to account for new presented facts.
