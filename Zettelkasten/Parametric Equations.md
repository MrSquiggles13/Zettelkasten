# Parametric Equations

## Notes

Parametric equations are a set of variables that are functions of a separate variable called the parameter $x = f(t)$ $y = g(t)$ $t = parameter$. The graphical representation of parametric equations is called the parametric curve and is traced in the direction that the parameter ($t$) increases which is called the orientation.

Parametric equations can be converted to their cartesian equivalent. One way is to set one function equal to $t$ and substituting $t$ into the other function $x = t^2$ $y = 2t - 3$ so $t = \frac{y+3}{2}$ and $x = (\frac{y + 3}{2})^2$ then values can be plugged in to determine the orientation. Another method involves removing the parameter $t$ by utilizing algebraic or trigonometric methods such as $x = sin(t)$ $y = cos^2(t)$ and since $sin^2(t) + cos^2(t) = 1$ then $x^2 + y = 1$ so the cartesian equivalent is $y = 1 - x^2$.

Differentiating a parametric equation involves taking the derivative of $y$ and $x$ separately as a quotient $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt}$ if $dx/dt \ne 0$ then the horizontal and vertical asymptotes can be found by setting $dy/dt = 0$ or $dx/dt = 0$ respectively. This can also help calculate a tangent line to the curve of a parametric equation by taking the derivative ($\frac{dy}{dx}$) and evaluating the points at $(x(t), y(t))$.
