# Quantum Mechanics

## Notes

A branch of physics that studies the wave nature of objects on the atomic scale.

Heisenberg's uncertainty principle states that it is impossible to know simultaneously the position and the momentum of a particle. For a particle of constant mass $\Delta x \cdot m \Delta u \ge \frac{h}{4\pi}$ where $\Delta x$ is the uncertainty in the position $\Delta u$ is the uncertainty in speed $m$ is the mass (which $m \Delta u = momentum$) and $h$ is Planck's constant. The more accurately we know the position (smaller $\Delta x$) the less accurately we know the speed (larger $\Delta u$) and vice versa. The best case scenario is the product of this uncertainty equation is equal to $\frac{h}{4 \pi}$.
