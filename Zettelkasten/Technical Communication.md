# Technical Communication

## Notes

A type of communication that helps explain complex topics or systems in a digestible and organized way. Different types of technical communication includes: project reports, presentations, interviews, journal publication, patent applications, user manuals, data sheets etc. This differs from standard communication as it has to e concise simple and complete. Normally collaborated and is user-centered while conveying information in a factual, quantitative manner.

It is important to know the audience or end user when preparing a technical document. Research is paramount to making sure the document is sound and without inconsistencies. A clear an concise outline should be used when making technical documents. Also the document should be revised multiple times to avoid errors and maintain professionality.

When giving a presentation it is important to speak with confidence, dress appropriately, engage with the audience and use effective body language and hand gestures. Should avoid filler words and turning the back on the audience or redirecting audience attention away from the presentation. Practicing the presentation can help build consistency and familiarity but over practice can make the presentation feel rigid and cold. Information should be relayed in a direct unambiguous manner, with a clean flow, and broken down in small chunks. Mistakes will happen and the presenter should do their best to handle them quickly and with poise. When practicing the presenter should simulate the actual presentation environment. It is good to keep in mind to speak slower than one thinks to keep a good pace.

Slide shows should be consistent in style and layout. A typical slideshow will have a Title, Introduction, Body, Conclusion/Summary, Appendix, and References slides. Figures should be numbered and have a brief description. Graphs must have its units labelled. Equations and formulas should have variables defined and be on one line.
