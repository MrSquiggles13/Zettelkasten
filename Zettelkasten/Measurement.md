# Measurement

## Notes

Measurement is identifying the quantity of a property or phenomenon using a defined known as reference.

There are seven fundamental or base units of measurement 
1. Mass - kilogram (kg) - amount of matter
2. Length - meter (m) - distance between two points
3. Time - second (s) - progression of existence
4. Temperature - kelvin (K) - how hot or cold something is
5. Amount of Substance - mole (mol) - quantity of particles
6. Electric Current - ampere (A) - strength of electric output
7. Luminous Intensity - candela (cd) - how bright a light source is

The meter is defined by the distance light travels in a vacuum in $\frac{1}{299,792,458}$ of a second.

The second is based on an atomic standard. The atomic clock measures the oscillations of microwave radiation absorbed by gaseous cesium atoms that are cooled to around 10<sup>-6</sup> K and one second is equivalent to 9,192,631,770 oscillations.

Temperature is relative to how hot or cold an object is compared to another object.

All other units are derived from these base units such as Volume which can be measure as a cubic dimension (m<sup>3</sup> or meters cubed) or a derived unit Liter (L) which a milliliter (mL) is equal to a centimeter cubed (cm<sup>3</sup>). Also density which is the relation of mass to volume (g/m<sup>3</sup> or grams per meter cubed).

The standard units of measurement that are universally accepted especially in the scientific community are called SI units. All the fundamental units are SI units and there are standard SI units for other derived measurements.

The Metric system is used for all SI units and for most of the world. The metric system has a base unit which can be scaled up and down my a factor prefix i.e. One kilometer is 1000 meters (kilo = 1000). 
These are called Metric Prefixes.

A mole (mol) is a unit of measurement for a collection of particles. One mole equals Avogadro's number of particles $1 \text{ mol} = 6.022 \times 10^{23}$. This is used as a conversion factor from moles of a substance to atoms and can calculate the amount of atoms of each element in a compound. Elements have a molar mass which is the amount of grams per one mole of that element and is the same as the atomic mass.

### Taking Measurements

Accuracy is the closeness of a measurement to the actual value.
Precision is the closeness of measurements in a series relative to each other.
![[Pasted image 20260518023824.png|334]]

Systematic error is when the values measures uniformly are higher or lower than the actual value and are normally due to a faulty measuring device.
Random error is any value that is outside the actual value and always occurs in varying degrees.

Calibration is the process of correcting a measuring device by comparing it to a known standard.

### Calculations Using Measurements

When measuring there is always a certain level of uncertainty in the accuracy of the measurement. The amount of digits in the quantity of a measurement that are certain are called Significant Figures. Significant Figures (or SigFigs for short) are certain to the last digit as this is the variable digit of uncertainty i.e. 230 could be anywhere from 225 - 234 as the last digit would have been rounded to account for the uncertainty.

SigFigs are typically defined by the zeros that appear in the measurement.
- All non-zero digits are significant - 523,484
- All zeros between non-zero digits are significant - 14,008 
- Leading zeros before first non zero digit are not significant - 0.023
- Trailing zeros are significant if decimal number - 1.3210
- Trailing zeros in non decimal are ambiguous and should be written in scientific notation - 5,280 -> (3 SigFigs) 5.28 X 10<sup>3</sup> OR (4 SigFigs) 5.280 X 10<sup>3</sup>

Significant figures are typically limited by the measuring device and the calculations that are done.
If a scale outputs to the thousandths place then the measurement is certain to that digit i.e. 23.043g cannot be written as 23.0430g

 When multiplying or dividing measurements the number with the least significant digits will be the value of significant digits for the final result i.e. 10.4 X 3.0 = 31 not 31.2
 
 When adding or subtracting the measurement with the least decimal places will be the same amount of decimal places for the output i.e. 16.3 + 1.083 = 17.4 not 17.383

Rounding is standard with ceiling on >= 5 and floor <5. When doing multiple calculations in a row values should be conserved and rounding plus SigFig reduction should be held off until final output.

Dimensional Analysis is the process of converting units into other units by treating them as variables and using conversion factors. When using conversion factors that are one dimensional and converting a 2 or 3 dimensional unit to another 2 or three dimensional unit the conversion factor must also be squared or cubed.
