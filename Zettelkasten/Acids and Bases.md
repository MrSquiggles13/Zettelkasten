# Acids and Bases

## Notes

Acids are molecular compounds and are not considered acids unless in an aqueous solution. Acids act like ionic compounds when in water and have a monatomic or polyatomic anion.
Oxoacids have oxygen molecules while Binary acids are just a hydrogen paired with another element. In an acid not all hydrogens are acidic.

Binary acids naming conventions start with hydro- and then the anion ending in -ic.
Oxoacids are named from the polyatomic anion and derived from the ionic name of the anion i.e. -ate becomes -ic and -ite become -ous.
![[Pasted image 20260518195400.png]]

Acids produce H<sup>+</sup> ions (protons) or H<sub>3</sub>O<sup>+</sup> (hydronium) when dissolved in water and strong acids will ionize completely while weak acids will dissociate very little.

Bases produce OH<sup>-</sup> ions when dissolved in water and strong bases will have either OH<sup>-</sup> or O<sup>2-</sup> as part of its structure and dissociate completely into ions in water while weak bases do not contain OH<sup>-</sup> ions but and electron pair on N which attracts H atoms in water to produce OH<sup>-</sup> and dissolve very little.
