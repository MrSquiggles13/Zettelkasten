# Teamwork in Engineering

## Notes

Teamwork is essential in any project and that definitely goes for the engineering world. When it comes to forming teams in general they normal form to meet two essential needs
	1. Productivity and Efficiency - More people can create more work output
	2. Innovation - Having diverse view points can help grant insights and perspectives to an issue
"Innovation comes from a collection of small hunches" - great quote
Teamwork is a skill that needs to be practiced.

Tuckman Model is a 4 stage model that describes how teams are typically formed
	1. Forming - When people come together and get to know each other and what they will work on
	2. Storming - Initial disagreements and friction where ideas are challenged and competition can form
	3. Norming - Structure and standards form to establish group dynamics
	4. Performing - Valuable output is produced and teamwork becomes more fluid
 ![[Pasted image 20260406013127.png|462]]
During these stages important milestones can be reached. Forming does not have much consequence but when the storming stage comes around is when teams can faulter. If a team lingers on this stage or cannot get past it the team becomes disjointed and fails. When the Norming stage is reached the team typically has a healthy dynamic with set expectations and is able to show real progress. It is key that by this stage the team has set fallbacks for any potential hang ups. Performing is when it all comes together and the flow-state is optimal. Teams are less focused on figuring themselves out and more on outputting effectively.

There are different types of roles in a team which can be informal or formal and can be good or bad. People can have multiple roles but important roles should not remain unfilled and roles should not overlap.

Norms are rules and expectations set by the team. Norms can be negative like always being late. it is important to set norms on how each other interact. The platform and setting should be agreed upon in addition how certain types of issues and conversations should be handled.
"Praise in public, criticize in private"

![[Pasted image 20260406013348.png]]
In a team it is important to establish early on. Groups can utilize many tools for scheduling, video calling, synchronous documents, text chats and much more. Focusing too much or having too many tools can cause distractions and hinder the effectiveness of the tools at hand causing the actual tasks that need to be done left on the way side.

Socialization is important in teams. It develops a connection outside of the project and work.

These are the 5 traits that successful teams normally have:
	1. Cooperation - Working together towards a common goal
	2. Communication - Relaying information clearly and effectively
	3. Coordination - Making sure that tasks are distributed and done in an efficient manner
	4. Consolation - Picking up another when they faulter, never leaving a teammate behind
	5. Conflict Resolution - Addressing conflict immediately and effectively
Is said that communication and consolation are the most important as the rest cannot exist without. When communicating one should agree and acknowledge another's idea then either add on to the idea or offer an alternative following.
"Expectations should not exceed a team members capabilities or constraints; we all have responsibilities and are only human"

Conflict can be a positive especially if it cultivates healthy debates and challenging ideas so they can be scrutinized effectively. "Apathy is the killer of team dynamics" having no conflict can be just as bad as having too much conflict. When dealing with conflict it is important to focus on the problem objectively instead of subjectively.  Creating alternatives that each party feels a part of can help the resolution feel fair "Decisions as Collaborations". When in a conflict it is important to keep in mind that oneself could be the cause of the conflict and to see their part in it. Asking what I want to accomplish vs what I want to say puts the problem in the frame of the goal instead of the self. Asking questions can lead to discussion and understand while making statements can leave the conversation one-sided.

"Good leaders will know when to be a follower to allow another's expertise shine through".
Proactive followership is when the lead expert for a problem at hand is able to dictate what needs to be done yet others are still actively apart of the solution process. Supporting the team whether as a follower or leader is key to fostering a strong inter-team connection.
