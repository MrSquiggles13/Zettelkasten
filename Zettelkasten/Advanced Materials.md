# Advanced Materials

## Notes

Advanced materials are substances that take on unique characteristics when compared to more common materials seen in nature.

Polymers are chains of molecules that link together where a unit molecule is called a monomer.

### Crystals

Crystalline structure is a type of solid structure where the molecules are highly ordered and organized while amorphous solid structures are random and unordered and polycrystalline having properties between the two.

Crystals have a crystalline structure in orderly three dimensional arrays of particles which forms a lattice which is made up of unit cells or repeating discrete units that are repeated and connected.

There are seven different types of crystalline structures with cubic being the most common. In these unit cells the atom arrangement has each atom fractionally contribute to the total atoms that occupy the space. Cubic unit cells have three varieties:
1. Primitive which has atoms on each corner of the cubic shape. Each of the corner atoms contribute 1/8 to the unit cells total atoms and with 8 corner atoms there is one atom in the unit cell.
2. Body centered which has an atom in the center of the cube and one on each corner. The corner atoms have the same contribution as the primitive but also has a whole atom in the center of the cube so there are two atoms in the unit cell. 
3. Face centered which has atoms on each corner and centered on each face of the cube. Same as primitive the corner atoms contribute one atom but the face centered atoms contribute 1/2 of their fractional part which for 6 faces that makes 3 atoms plus the corner fraction contribution there are four atoms in the unit cell.

When atoms pack together it is normally done as efficiently as possible to minimize empty space. Most metals pack in a way called closest-packing which has two different forms, a hexagonal close packing where layers alternate in a pattern where every other aligns vertically with an ABAB pattern, and cubic-closest packing where the pattern alternates in three forms vertically shifting in a triagonal pattern ABCABC.

Some alloys have one metallic compound fill the spaces in between another metallic compounds lattice structure.

Ionic solids have a unit cell where the ratio of cations to anions is the same as the compounds empirical formula. The smaller ions (typically cations) will fit in the spacing of the larger ions (typically anions) lattice structure.

### Conductors

Band theory is an extension of [[Molecular Orbital Theory]] which helps explain how solid elemental metals have delocalized electrons. The structural arrangement of metallic solids' electrons shows that there are two bands of orbitals the valence band which holds the valence electrons and the conduction band made of bonding and antibonding orbitals that have no inherent electrons. When depicting a solid elemental metal such as Lithium Li, the element by itself has a molecular orbital diagram with a fairly large gap between the valence and conduction band, but due to the nature of metals a solid piece of Li will have many atoms sharing their electrons almost like a diatomic elements but to an extremely high degree. For Li<sub>x</sub> as x gets to a really high number the gap between the energy levels of valence and conduction bands essentially disappears which can allows the valence band electrons to jump up to the conduction band orbitals and become delocalized.

Conductors are typically metals that have no energy gap between their valence and conduction bands allowing high conductivity except when heated since the randomness of the movement of atoms disrupts the delocalized electrons flow.
Semiconductors are typically metalloids and have a small energy gap between their valence and conduction bands which allows a small amount of current to flow but will increase conductivity when heated since this essentially shrinks the gap between bands.
Insulators are typically non-metals and have a large energy gap between their valence and conduction bands which allows no current even when heated.
The greater the electronegativity difference of a compound the larger the band gap.

Semiconductor compounds have an average of 4 valence electrons between their elemental atoms.

Super conductors are materials that can conduct current with no loss of energy due to the resistive heating of colliding electrons which typically creates electrical energy loss to frictional heat energy.

Doping is the infusing of impurity atoms on a material to increase conductivity. This is typically done on semi conductors and can increase conductivity by a million fold with a small amount of impurity atoms infused. 
This process create two types of semiconductors:
- n-type is made by infusing atoms with more than four valence electrons into the semiconductive material which creates a negative charge as the electrons jump to the conduction band and become delocalized.
- p-type is made by infusing atoms with less than four valence electrons into the semiconductive material which creates a positive charge from the positive holes of the added impurity which are filled by the semiconductive materials electrons creating positive pockets of charge.

When an n-type and a p-type semiconductor are placed together they form an n-p junction. This can have many applications like a diode which only lets current to flow one way since the positive side can only be hooked up to a positive terminal and negative with negative, and these are used in LEDs (Light Emitting Diodes) and many other [[Electrical Components]]. There are also rectifiers which are converters of AC to DC since the junction will only let the current flow one way and negate the back pulse of the AC.

### Nanomaterials

Nanomaterials are made of very small particles 1-100 nm long and differ greatly from macroparticles. Semiconductors cannot conduct electricity until they are 10-100 atoms across or about 1-10 nm because their bands are not able to be close enough. This grouping of 10-100 atoms or 1-10 nm is called a quantum dot.

As the particle size increases the band gap decreases which causes a color change. This color change can help determine how much nanomaterial concentration is in a solution. The band gap of the solution is calculated by the color emitted when the solution has a UV light shining on it since this excites the electrons and emits energy by electromagnetic radiation.

Carbon nanotubes are sheets of carbon rolled into a cylinder with half sphere (C<sub>60</sub> cut in half) capped on the end and can be either a semiconductor or a conductor depending on the diameter. The dimensions are typically around 1000nm long and 1nm wide.

### Liquid Crystal

Liquid crystal is a material that has properties of a liquid like the ability to flow while also having the properties of a crystalline solid such as a high degree of order at the molecular level.

Isotropic is the physical properties of a substance that are the same in every direction within a phase and is true for most gases liquids and amorphous solids due to the lack of an irregular lattice.

Anisotropic is the physical properties withing a phase of a substance that are dependent on the direction like in a crystalline structure.

Liquid crystals are anisotropic for several properties like electrical and optical.
