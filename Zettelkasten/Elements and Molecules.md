Elements are the fundamental building blocks of matter and are referred to as atoms in physical form. Atom is derived from the Greek Atomos which means indivisible (coined by Democritus).

Elements are either metals, non-metals, or metalloids. The stair step line in the periodic table separates the metals from non-metals and the elements that touch the line are metalloids.'

Metals are shiny solids at room temperature (except mercury Hg which is a liquid), are good conductors of heat and electricity and are malleable and ductile i.e. shaped easily without breaking.

Metalloids are shiny but brittle solids at room temperature, semiconductors of electricity and heat, and have intersecting properties of metals and non-metals.

Non-metals are either gases or brittle solids at room temperature (except bromine Br which is a liquid), are poor conductors of electricity and heat, and are not malleable or ductile.

Most Elements are metals and are solid at normal conditions (25°C at 1 atm) but normally exist in nature as compounds combined with other elements. Some metals are found uncombined like copper Cu, silver Ag, gold Au and platinum Pt. Carbon appears in vast nearly pure deposits of coal.

Metallic properties and behavior typically increases down a group. The Ionization energy decreases so potential to lose an electron to form a cation increases as can be seen in group 15 when the top elements are more likely to be anions and the bottom one to be cations. Metallic properties and behaviors typically decreases across a period towards the right. Ionization energy increases and size decreases while electron affinity becomes more negative which increases the tendency for atoms to gain an electron and form anions.

Elements are represented by element symbols which are typically the first or first couple letters of the elements name. Some element symbols are representative of what the element was called previously i.e. Iron (Fe) was called Ferrum

Molecules are 2 or more atoms that are bonded together i.e. H<sub>2</sub>O.  Molecules can have different types of atoms bonded which is called a compound. Compounds have different elements but are of constant composition. A compound can be decomposed to its elements through chemical reactions. 

Molecules with the same type of atoms is called an element. Element can refer to either atoms or molecules of one type and a few common ones are O<sub>2</sub>, N<sub>2</sub> and S<sub>8</sub>.

Some elements exist naturally as diatomic molecules which are 2 atoms of the same element are bonded together which are
- N<sub>2</sub> - Nitrogen
- O<sub>2</sub> - Oxygen
- F<sub>2</sub> - Fluorine
- Cl<sub>2</sub> - Chlorine
- Br<sub>2</sub> - Bromine
- I<sub>2</sub> - Iodine

The noble gases are stable and occur in air as separate elemental atoms.

A substance is classified as consisting of either an element or a compound. Pure substances consist of only one type of element or compound

Fuel is a molecular organic compound that reacts with O<sub>2</sub> to release energy.

### Periodic Table

The Periodic Table is a visual representation of known elements and is sectioned into groups (columns) and periods (rows). Groups have similar properties and periods transition through these different groups. 
Some special groups are:
- Group IA - Alkali Metals
- Group IIA - Alkaline Earth Metals
- Groups 3-12 (IIIB- VIIIB and IB-IIB) - Transition Metals
- Group VIIA - Halogens
- Group VIIA - Noble Gases

The elements on the periodic table transition from metals starting at bottom left to non-metals towards the top right.

Groups 1, 2, and 13-18 are called the main group elements. Groups 3-12 are called the transition elements/metals and the two isolated rows underneath the table are called the inner transition elements/metals.

Isomers are compounds with the same molecular formula and molar mass but have different physical and chemical properties.
### Size and Trends

Bond length or atomic size of an atom is the measure of the distance between two bonded atoms nuclei divided by two. This relative measure is due to atoms not having a solid surface and can vary from substance to substance. Ionic radii is similar but in ionic compounds. Typically cations shrink from their neutral state from the loss of electrons making the nucleus's pull stronger and pulling the electrons closer to it. Anions normally grow since the addition of an electron causes more repulsion from the nucleus and shielding of the other electrons. Metallic radii is the same as atomic radii but in a crystal and covalent radii is between mostly non-metals covalent molecules. Atomic size for neutral atoms trend from small to large going from the top right of the periodic table towards the bottom left while ionic sizes are split from left (cations) and right (anions) where cations are always smaller than their isoelectric anion counterpart but cations and anions compared to themselves trend smaller going across the period and larger down a group like neutral atoms. Isoelectric ions are ions of different element that have the same number of electrons. Knowing the radii of certain elements can help determine the radii of others such as Cl<sub>2</sub> is just the total distance in half for one Cl atom then that can be used to measure Na radii in a NaCl ionic compound since Cl is known and be subtracted from the total distance of the bond.
