# Chemical Bonds

## Notes

There are three types of general elemental bonding methods which are called chemical bonds
1. Ionic bonds - electrons are transferred from one atom to another of different elements. Typically between a metal and a non metal or polyatomic ion.
2. Covalent bonds - electrons are shared between atoms of elements. These make discrete molecules or polyatomic ions.
3. Metallic bonds - metallic atoms align and share a pool of delocalized electrons and is typical with elements with more metallic characteristics.

In general atoms bond together because bonding lowers the potential energy between negative and positive particles.

Metallic bonds are due to the outer electron shell being well shielded by the inner core electrons and the atoms being fairly large, this makes them lose electrons quite easily and not readily gain electrons. Delocalized electrons are electrons that are not centralized to a specific bond or orbital but "float" freely between bonds and orbitals. This allows metals to be ductile and malleable since the atoms can shift past each other without affecting the internal structure or attractive forces of the substance. Metals also have a moderately high melting point since the atoms in a metal can move freely without affecting the internal attractive forces but they have an extremely high boiling point as there is a strong resistance to break away from the electron pool. This electron pool also affects metal conductivity and heat transfer as the electrons in the metal are delocalized and can shift around to transfer energy quite easily.

Covalent bonds have a low melting point while having a strong bond energy because the intermolecular forces which is the strength of attraction between separate discrete molecules is very weak. Network covalent solids have covalent bonds between the discrete molecules throughout the substance which makes the structure very strong since covalent bonds are so strong. This can be shown in diamond which is a network covalent solid and the strongest substance known to man.  

These bonds are not concrete but a gradient to how ionic covalent or metallic a bond is. While metallic relies on the characteristics of the elements bonded, covalent and ionic bonds are graded by the difference in electronegativity of the atoms. Electronegativity is the measure of how strongly an element attracts bonding electrons. Electronegativity has a trend on the periodic table from bottom left being the least electronegative towards the top right with the most electronegative elements. This can create polar bonds where one atom has a stronger pull on the shared electrons while non-polar bonds have equal pull on the shared electrons. Polar bonds will have a partial charge denoted by $\delta$ with either a plus for less pull or a minus for more pull and is determined by the more electronegative atom which will have a partial negative charge. The greater the difference in electronegativity the more polar a bond will be. Bond strength is dependent on the bond length and in ionic compounds the magnitude of the ion charges. Partial ionic character is how ionic a bond is and is proportional to the difference in electronegativity. The difference in electronegativity can be measure from 0-3.3 (highest possible from ionic compound CsF) or a percentage where 50% electronegativity difference is the divide between covalent and ionic compounds but no bonds have 100% since every bond shares electrons even if only very slightly.

Lattice energy is the energy required to separate one mole of a solid ionic compound into its gaseous ions. The Born-Haber cycle is a thermodynamic application of Hess's law which is used to calculate the lattice energy. Lattice energy rarely can be measured directly so this cycle uses different reactions and their change in enthalpy of formation with the steps to take an ionic solid separate its ions excite those ions into a gas phase and then split them into their individual atoms. Lattice energy is strongly related to a compounds bond strength which is measured by Coulombs Law $E_{el} = \frac{kQ_1Q_2}{d}$ where $E_{el}$ is electrostatic potential energy $k$ is coulombs constant $Q$ is the charge of the ion and $d$ is the bond distance. This shows that lattice energy is proportional to ion charge but inversely proportional to the ion radii. Lattice energy affects many aspects of ionic compounds such as melting point where a higher energy means a higher melting point and is proportional and also solubility where a strong lattice energy has a lower solubility since the compound does not readily separate into its ions.

Ionic solids only exists because their lattice energy far exceeds the total amount of energy needed to form its separate ions. Ionic compounds are brittle because when a force of high energy is applied the ions in the structure shift which causes ions of same polarity to repel each other and this repulsion causes a separation of the ions or on a macroscopic level a crack in the substance. Vaporized ionic compounds consist of ion pairs or gaseous ionic molecules not individual ions.

Lewis dot structure is a diagram model that represents the bonds (dashes) and valence electrons (dots) of elements (symbol). When drawing a Lewis structure the element that can form the most bonds typically is the central element.
The steps for drawing a Lewis structure are:
1. Write out the atomic skeleton lining out the different elements with the least electronegative in the center
2. Sum up the valence electrons of all the atoms and notate the total
3. Place a single bond between each pair of atoms
4. Add the remaining electrons to make an octet (or 2 for H) of each outer atom
5. Add any remaining electrons to the central atom to obtain octet if possible
6. If the central atom does not have an octet shift unpaired electrons of outer atoms to make double or triple bonds

Octet rule states that molecules will share electrons so gain stability like that of a noble gas by having a full shell (mostly 8 sometimes 2 like He). For polyatomic ions the total amount of valence electrons will be adjusted by the charge of the ion (VE - ion charge). Exceptions to the octet rule are incomplete octets where there are less than 8 valence electrons, odd number of octets where total valence electrons is an odd number and cannot be dispersed evenly to meet the 8 required, and expanded octet/hypervalent where the central atom (typically a non metal of period 3 or higher since they have a d orbital available) has more than 8 electrons. Odd number octets can form free radicals which are molecules or atoms that have one or more unpaired electrons. Free radicals are extremely reactive and can bond to H in biomolecules and extract them to form new free radicals which continues in a cycle call free-radical proliferation that can disrupt genes and membranes.

Formal charge is the charge that each bonded atom would have if electrons were shared equally. This metric is not a real charge but a tool to help with calculating bond structure and is calculated by $FC = VE_{total} - 1/2BE_{total} - NBE_{total}$ where FC is formal charge VE is valence electrons BE is bonded electrons and NBE is non-bonded electrons. Dominant Lewis structures have a formal charge of the lowest magnitude or if the magnitudes are equal then the most negative charge should be on the most electronegative atom.

Resonance is when the arrangement of electron pairing is not adequately described by a single Lewis structure.

Bond order is the magnitude of the bonds between atoms and is denoted by the amount of shared electrons in the bond where a single bond is a bond order of one a double bond is two and triple bond is three. This correlates with the strength of the bond as more bonds mean a shorter bond length between atoms which in turn make bond energy increase. For molecules with resonance in their structure the bond order of those partial bonds is calculated by number of electrons in bond pairs divided by the number of pairs of bonded atoms.

It is widely debated and proven to not be reliable whether the octet rule or formal charge is the proper way to classify molecular bonds and has shown through experimentation that molecules will favor sometimes one or the other.

Bonds are a balance of the nuclei attracting electrons but nuclei and electrons resisting each other. When a bond is formed the separate atoms will bond too close and revert to equilibrium distance which will cause energy to be released. Breaking a bond is always and endothermic process while forming a bond is always an exothermic process. 

The heat released or absorbed during chemical changes is due to the differences between reactant bond energies and product bond energies. The enthalpy of formation for reactions are calculated by finding the energy absorbed by breaking the bonds of the reactants forming separate atoms and the energy released by forming the products out of the separated atoms and summating the two sums. $\Delta H°_{rxn} = \sum\Delta H°_{reactant \ bonds \ broken} + \sum\Delta H°_{product \ bonds \ formed}$ The entire reaction will be exothermic if the energy of formed products is greater than reactants broken or endothermic if the energy of reactants broken is greater than the products formed. This can also be calculated with bond energy $\Delta H°_{rxn} = \sum BE_{reactant \ bonds \ broken} - \sum BE_{product \ bonds \ formed}$ The process involves calculating the energy of breaking all of the reactants bonds, then the energy to form all the products from the atoms of the broken bonds, then summating them with the appropriate signs but making sure physical state does not change as that affects the amount of energy.

### Molecule Shape and Structure

Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion (VSEPR) theory is a model used to predict the three dimensional geometry of molecules based on the electrostatic repulsion of valence electrons since repulsion is minimized by an ideal distance making the structure more stable.

Electron Domains (EDs) are groups of valence electrons around an atom and can be either lone unbonded pairs, or single, double, or triple bonds. 
Electron Domain Geometries or Parent Structures are classifications of the shape from the electron configuration around a central atom which is the number of EDs that surround it. Two EDs is a linear configuration, three EDs is trigonal planar, four EDs is tetrahedral, five EDs is trigonal bipyramidal, and six EDs is octahedron.

Molecular shape is the configuration of atoms around a central atom and does not include but is affected by unbonded electron pairs. Molecular shape is dependent on the parent structure and the amount of EDs that are lone pairs, and if none is just the electron domain geometry standard shape. 

In ascending order of amount of lone pairs: 
- Linear parent structure has just a linear molecular shape
- Trigonal planar has trigonal planar and bent
- Tetrahedral has tetrahedral, trigonal pyramidal, and bent
- Trigonal bipyramidal has seesaw, T-shaped, and linear
- Octahedral has square pyramidal and square planar.

Each of these parent structures has an ideal angle when all EDs are single bonds:
- Linear 180°
- Trigonal Planar 120°
- Tetrahedral 109.5°
- Trigonal Bipyramidal  equatorial = 120° axial = 90°
- Octahedral 90°

These bond angles are affected by any lone pairs in the electron geometry as they are more localized to the central atoms nucleus and create more repulsion. As more lone pairs are a part of the structure the less the bond angle will be from the ideal. Double bonds also can have more repulsion since the electron density field is more spread out and the bond length is shorter than a single bond. Triple bonds do not affect the structure as triple bonds are only in linear formations. Resonance around a central atom will have a bond order of some fractional value distributed to each bond so the ideal bond angle will not be affected by it alone.

When there are more than one central atom the molecular structure can be determined by calculating the bond angles and structure of each central atom individually.

The VSEPR theory steps for determining a molecular shape are:
- Draw a Lewis structure of the molecule
- Assign a parent structure based on EDs
- Predict bond angles based on lone pairs or double bonds
- Name molecular group from the shape those bond angles would make

### Molecular Polarity

The polarity of a molecule is determined by the net vector of the summation of bond polarities and their direction in relation to the molecular shape. This means that a partial charge is more favored on a certain side of a molecule called a dipole moment. Dipole moment ($\mu$) is the measure of the molecular polarity and it has units called Debye (D) which are derived from coulombs and length $1D = 3.34 \cdot 10^{-30} C \cdot m$. When a molecule has polar bonds but they cancel each other out or the vectors summate to zero then the molecule itself is non polar. Polarity affects intermolecular forces which are the electrostatic attraction between distinct molecules and affects certain properties such as boiling point and density.

A way to determine the molecular polarity is to:
- Draw a Lewis structure
- Determine the parent structure and molecular shape
- Calculate the bond polarities and determine if the direction and strength creates a dipole moment

Any molecules of just carbon and hydrogen are always non-polar.
Molecular shape contribute to a lot of biochemical processes such as taste buds on the tongue which are protein receptors that only certain molecules can fit into because of their shape like sucrose in a "sweet" taste bud also neurons only fit in certain receptors in the brain.
