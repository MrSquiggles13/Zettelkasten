## Notes

Matter is anything with a mass and a volume. The fundamental chemical components and arrangements that make up all physical substances is called the composition of matter.

The amount of matter that an object consists of is called mass. Most matter typically occurs as mixtures

## States of Matter

- **Solid**: has a definite shape and volume with tightly compacted atoms or molecules with a strong attraction
- **Liquid**: loosely connected atoms or molecules with a weak attraction and can slide past each other. Takes the shape of a container up to its volume.
- **Gas**: Atoms and molecules has little to no attraction to each other. Takes the shape and volume of its container and does not have a surface. 

States of matter can be changed through temperature and pressure changes.
The six fundamental state changes are
- **Melting**: Solid to Liquid
- **Freezing**: Liquid to Solid
- **Evaporating**: Liquid to Gas
- **Condensation**: Gas to Liquid
- **Deposition**: Gas to Solid
- **Sublimation**: Solid to Gas

Physical properties of matter are the characteristics a substance shows itself like mass, volume and color.
Chemical properties of matter shows as it changes into or interacts with other substances i.e. flammable or corrosive. 
Physical changes only affect the physical properties of the object like changing states of matter or cutting a piece of wood in half.
Chemical changes are when one or more substances changes into one or more substance and alters their composition and properties i.e. separating water (H<sub>2</sub>O) into hydrogen (H) and oxygen (O).

Extensive properties are dependent on the amount of substance i.e. mass
Intensive properties are independent of the amount of substance i.e. density

### Gas properties

Gases behave very differently that other physical states because of the large capacity of space between the particles and very low density. This makes gases compressible and expandable as the volume of a gas is dependent on its container along with temperature pressure and moles. Gases also mix in any proportions to form solutions unlike other states.

Pressure is the measure of force (gas particles hitting the walls of a container) over the area the force is applied to. Pressure is measured in atmospheres (atm), torr, millimeters of mercury (mmHg), pounds per square inch (psi - lb/in<sup>2</sup>), and Pascals (Pa - N/m<sup>2</sup>) and a few conversions are 1 torr = 1 mmHg, 1 atm = 760 torr and 1 atm = 101,325 Pascals. Atmospheric pressure in the constant force of the atmosphere near the Earth's crust and it makes pressures in containers relevant. Typically the pressure of the atmosphere equals the pressure of a container especially if it is open but if the pressure in a closed container deviates from the atmospheric pressure this can cause physical changes of the container or gaseous substance inside. Gases in the Earth's atmosphere vary in physical properties as altitude increases which decreases the temperature and pressure of the gases. A barometer measures the pressure of the atmosphere using mercury in am evacuated tube with excess mercury at its base so the mercury will rise and fall in relevance to the external atmospheric pressure.

In a sealed container as pressure is inversely proportional to volume since the reduction of volume will cause the particles to strike the container walls more frequently as there is less distance for them to travel and vice versa. This is a part of Boyle's law which states that at a constant temperature (T) volume (V) = constant (k) x 1/pressure(P). This can be used to compare initial and final changes in pressure and volume from the equation $P_1V_1 = P_2V_2$ and can be used to solve for an unknown.

Under constant pressure as temperature decreases pressure also decreases since the particles slow down and do not strike the container walls as frequently and vice versa. This is part of Charles's law which states at constant pressure (P) that volume (V) = constant (k) x temperature (T). This can be used to compare initial and final changes in volume and temperature $\frac{V_1}{T_1} = \frac{V_2}{T_2}$ and can be used to solve for an unknown. Also when gases cool their density increases because their volume decreases as the particles slow down. 

Boyle's and Charles's laws can be combined to create the combined gas law which is $\frac{P_1V_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2V_2}{T_2}$.

Under constant pressure and temperature as moles of particles decrease so does the volume due to less particles taking up space. This is Avogadro's law which states that at constant pressure (P) and temperature (T) that volume (V) = constant (k) x moles (n). This can be used to compare initial and final changes in volume and moles $\frac{V_1}{n_1} = \frac{V_2}{n_2}$ and can be used to solve for an unknown.

The combined gas law can be further combined with Avogadro's law to have the super combined gas law $\frac{P_1V_1}{n_1T_1} = \frac{P_2V_2}{n_2T_2}$ where a constant can be derived for all four properties which is the ratio $\frac{PV}{nT} = constant = R$ where R is called the gas constant. This is used to derive the ideal gas law which is $PV = nRT$ where $R = 0.08206 \frac{L \cdot atm}{K \cdot mol}$. These gas laws are for ideal gases which do not exist but most gases behave very close to ideal under STP.

Standard Temperature and Pressure (STP) is a base line condition for gases to measure their properties with controlled variables. STP is temperature = 273.15 K and pressure = 1.00 atm.

Molar volume ($\frac{V}{n}$) is the measure of volume per mole of gas under specific conditions of temperature and pressure. The molar volume of any ideal gas at STP is $22.414 \frac{L}{mol}$.

The ideal gas law can be rearranged to find the density of gases since $n = \frac{mass}{MolarMass}$ so $\frac{m}{V} = density = \frac{PMM}{RT}$. This shows that density is directly proportional to its molar mass and inversely proportional to temperature and proportional to pressure. This can also derive how to find the molar mass $MM = \frac{mRT}{PV}$.

Suction is not the drawing of a liquid up an apparatus but the liquid being pushed up the apparatus due to the pressure difference.

Zero Kelvin was discovered through the observation of comparing the change of a gasses temperature to its volume. It was noticed that almost all gasses when taking the regression line of this ratio had an intercept of 0 K when at 0 volume which is not possible for mass to have 0 volume so the concept of absolute zero was derived.

Since Kelvin is a direct proportion to volume Celsius and Fahrenheit cannot be used for gas equations as it will not scale properly to account for the negative values.

When a reaction results in a lower amount of moles of product than reactants when volume and pressure are constant then temperature must increase due to the energy release of the reaction (which is exothermic) not able to go anywhere and converts to thermal energy that heats the container. 

Absent of chemical reactions gases in a mixture will behave as if they are the only gas present in the solution. Dalton's law of partial pressures states that in a mixture of non reacting gases the total pressure is the sum of the partial pressures of each individual gas. When gases are in the same container partial pressures can be used to find the total pressure of the container $P_{total} = \frac{\sum_{x=gas}^{amount \ gasses}(n_x)RT}{V}$.

Real gases have mass and volume and do attract and repel other particles. Gases normally act very closely to that of an ideal gas but under extreme conditions like high pressure and temperature gases tend to deviate drastically from the ideal. Ideal gases have a constant ratio comparing their properties given one mole of gas $n = 1$ $\frac{PV}{RT} = 1$ but real gases will deviate from this constant ratio when in extreme conditions. Interparticle attractions while typically negligible at STP when at extreme pressure and temperature the attractions become more prevalent. As external pressure rises and volume decreases particles get closer together and will attract other particles in motion which causes less collision frequency and lowers the pressure. As temperature is lowered particles will slow down and attract each other for longer periods. At extreme pressure free volume space between particles becomes less than the container volume which makes the particles volume a large contributor to the space cause a sharp increase in its ideal ratio $\frac{PV}{RT} \gt \gt 1$.

Van der Waal's equation $(P+\frac{an^2}{V^2})(V - bn) = nRT$ accounts for these deviations through the Van der Waal constants where $a$ relates to the number and distribution of electrons to account for interparticle attractions and $b$ which relates to a particles volume. At ordinary conditions Van der Waal's equation is equivalent to the ideal gas equation since $a \approx 0$ and $b \approx 0$ since particle attraction and volume are negligible so $P+\frac{an^2}{V^2} \approx P$ and $V - nb \approx V$.

### Kinetic Molecular Theory

The kinetic molecular theory of gases is a model that explains the observations of gases under normal conditions based on their behavior at the molecular level. This is based on a few assumptions: 
1. Gas particles are small with negligible volume compared to the volume of its container so they are considered points of mass or zero volume. 
2. Gas particles do not attract or repel each other.
3. Gas particles move in straight lines between collisions and collisions are elastic meaning the energy of the collision is transferred completely and none is lost through friction.
4. Total pressure is due to the sum of collision force on container.
5. The average kinetic energy of a gas is dependent only on absolute temperature change.

All gases have the same average kinetic energy at the same temperature making average kinetic energy constant when temperature is constant. Kinetic energy is calculated $KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$ and since KE is constant at the same temp for all gases their mass and velocity are inversely proportional to each other hence a lighter gas will have a higher average velocity and vice versa. The proportion of kinetic energy to temperature KE = constant x T is used to derive the formula $KE = \frac{3}{2}(\frac{R}{N_a})^{-k}T$ where N<sub>a</sub> is Avogadro's number. Using the kinetic energy formula for particle motion in a large population $KE = \frac{1}{2}mu^2$ where u is the average speed, can be set equal to the previous formula $\frac{3}{2}(\frac{R}{N_a})^{-k}T = KE = \frac{1}{2}mu^2$ and solving for u while also using the equivalent for molar mass $N_am = MM$ the equation for u is $u =\sqrt{\frac{3RT}{MM}}$ which is called the root mean squared speed. In this equation $u = \frac{m}{s}$ $R = 8.314 \frac{J}{mol \cdot K}$ since $J = \frac{kg \cdot m^2}{s^2}$ and $MM = \frac{kg}{mol}$.

Diffusion is the movement of gas particles from a high concentration to a lower or through another gas and sometimes liquids and solids. Effusion is the passage of particles through a microscopic hole. The rate of effusion and diffusion is proportional to velocity and inversely proportional to mass or $rate \propto \frac{1}{MM}$. The rates of two gases are related by $\frac{rate_a}{rate_b} = \frac{\sqrt{3RTMM_b}}{\sqrt{3RTMM_a}} = \sqrt{\frac{MM_a}{MM_a}}$ at constant temperature. Diffusion is much slower than effusion because of the impedance of other particles slowing down velocity.

Mean free path is the average distance a particle travels between collisions at constant temperature and pressure. Collision frequency is the probable speed over the mean free path which calculates the average collisions per second where collisions are with other particles and container walls.
### Laws of Mass

1. The law of mass conservation state that the total mass of substances do not change during a chemical reaction. The number of substances may change but total amount of amount will not. Matter cannot be created or destroyed but Einstein found that some mass can be converted to energy or vice versa during some reactions but the amount is so small that it is basically negligible. (when Carbon is burned $3.6 \cdot 10^{10^{10}} \%$ of its mass is unaccounted for which is converted to energy)
2. The law of definite proportions states that no matter the source of a particular compound it will be composed of the same elements in the same parts by mass. These parts can be represented by mass fractions which is the part of the compounds mass that each element contributes to $\text{Mass Fraction} = \frac{\text{mass of element}}{\text{mass of whole compound}}$ and the mass percentage is the mass fraction expressed as a percentage $\text{Mass Percent} = \left( \frac{\text{mass of element}}{\text{mass of whole compound}} \right) \times 100\%$.
3. The law of multiple proportions states that if element A and element B can react to form two separate compounds the different masses of B that combine with the fixed mass of A can be expressed as a ratio of whole numbers. This states that compound made of the same type of elements but differ in composition will relate to each other in a non fractional manner. $\frac{\text{Mass of O in CO}_2}{\text{Mass of O in CO}} = \frac{32.00\text{ g}}{16.00\text{ g}} = \frac{2}{1} = 2:1$
