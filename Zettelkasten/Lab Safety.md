# Lab Safety

## Notes

Lab accidents can result in fire, chemical burns, cuts, damage to the eyes, damage to personal property or equipment, and sometimes death.

Personal protective equipment (PPE) helps prevent lab accidents from causing injury or damage and include lab coats, long pants or skirt, gloves, close-toe close-heel shoes, and splash proof goggles or safety glasses. In more hazardous environments additional PPE might be used such as face shields, respirators, and specialized protective clothing.

Chemistry labs have special equipment such as chemical flame hoods, eye wash stations, showers, fire extinguishers of the correct type, fire blankets and chemical spill kits.

Most labs require that reagents (stored chemicals) are labeled appropriately with identity, concentration of solution, and hazard type. Chemicals have associated Safety Data Sheets (SDS) that provide information such as properties, hazard risks, and protective and first-aid measures.

Chemicals should never be poured down the drain but properly labeled and disposed of in the correct manner and place.
