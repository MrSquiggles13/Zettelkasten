# Valence Bond Theory

## Notes

### Valence Bond Theory

Valence bond theory is the assumption that a covalent bond is formed between two atoms by the overlap of half-filled valence atomic orbitals. This is based on the overlapping orbitals having their wave functions in phase which increases the amplitude between nuclei. These newly formed orbitals are called hybrid orbitals and are a mix between standard orbitals and is labelled by the orbitals that made them i.e. s + p = sp hybrid orbital and s + p + p = sp<sup>2</sup> hybrid orbital. The amount of hybrid orbitals formed will always equal the amount of standard orbitals it took to make them. 

These hybrid orbitals help account for the molecular shapes that are formed since standard orbitals could not allow such configurations. Each parent structure is the formation of a certain number of hybrid orbitals: Linear = two sp hybrid orbitals, Trigonal planar = three sp<sup>2</sup> hybrid orbitals, Tetrahedral = four sp<sup>3</sup> hybrid orbitals. Even if a hybrid orbital is not technically needed to share electrons they are formed anyway to accommodate the molecular shape's bond angles.

The space formed by overlapping orbitals will follow Pauli's exclusion principle where a maximum of two electrons can occupy the orbital and they must have opposite spins. This overlap is maximized as the closer the electrons are to the nuclei the stronger and more optimal the bond.

When hybridization occurs if there is not enough unpaired electrons in the orbitals a paired electron is put into an excited state and promoted to a higher energy level orbital i.e. 2s<sup>2</sup>2p<sup>2</sup> has 3 unpaired in the p orbital but paired electrons in the s orbital. If there is four bonds being made (like carbon to four hydrogens C -> H<sub>4</sub>) then an electron in the 2s orbital is put into an excited state and promoted to the 2p orbital where four sp<sup>3</sup> orbitals are formed with four unpaired electrons that can now be bonded (overlap orbitals with another atom). If the molecule formed will result in the atom have lone pairs the s orbital does not need to be excited and the hybrid orbitals can b formed directly such as H<sub>2</sub>O where the oxygen molecule 2s<sup>2</sup>2p<sup>4</sup> has two unpaired electrons than can bond with the hydrogen and localizes the other two sets of paired unbonded electrons into their own sp<sup>3</sup> orbitals after they are formed.

Hybridization does not apply to large nonmetal hydrides since their bond angles when measure experimentally are 92° which is very close to the angles of perpendicular p orbitals. This is due to the fact that the bond length is so large the repulsive forces are very weak and hybridization is unnecessary for optimal bond configuration. d orbital hybrids are also not as accurate since d orbitals have such high energy that they are not able to hybridize with s and p orbital effectively.

A sigma ($\sigma$) bond is the overlap of orbitals along the intermolecular axis. A pi ($\pi$) bond is the sideways overlap of p orbitals above and below the intermolecular axis. Single bonds are always sigma bonds and pi bonds only happen in double or triple bonds. A sigma bond is typically between hybridized orbitals or a hybridized orbital and an s orbital. A pi bond is the sideways overlap of two unhybridized p orbitals. Doble bonds are a sigma and a pi bond while triple bonds are a sigma and two pi bonds.

Molecules with resonance have delocalized pi bonding distributed through all the unhybridized p orbitals involved in the partial bonds. Sigma bonds are always localized yet pi bonds can be localized or delocalized. Graphene is able to conduct electricity because of the large delocalized pi bonds over the many partial bonds that make up graphene which makes the electrons in those orbitals delocalized and easier to transfer throughout the substance.

Although a double bond is less than twice the strength of a single bond (since sigma bonds are stronger than pi bonds) the convention of bond order for double being two and single being one still works.

Sigma bonds by itself in an atomic bond allows for free rotation of the atoms along the bond axis. Pi bonds restrict this movement as the p orbitals have to be parallel to overlap effectively. This gives form to trans and cis structures which are compounds of the same elemental make up but different atomic arrangement which can affect certain properties in the molecule.
